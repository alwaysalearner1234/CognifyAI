Newton's Laws of Motion. First law (inertia): an object retains its state of rest or uniform motion unless acted upon by a net external force. Second law: F = ma; net force equals mass times acceleration, direction of force is direction of acceleration. Third law (action-reaction): for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction acting on different bodies. Example: a 2 kg block pushed with 10 N net force accelerates at 5 m/s^2. Free-body diagrams show all forces: weight mg downward, normal N upward, applied and friction forces horizontally.

Friction. Static friction opposes impending motion, max value mu_s * N; kinetic friction opposes actual motion, value mu_k * N with mu_k < mu_s. On an inclined plane of angle theta, normal N = mg cos theta and downhill component mg sin theta. Motion starts when mg sin theta exceeds mu_s mg cos theta. Rough surfaces increase friction; lubrication reduces it. Example diagram: block on incline with friction arrow up the slope.

Work, Energy and Power. Work W = F d cos theta; work-energy theorem: net work equals change in kinetic energy (W_net = KE_final - KE_initial). Conservative forces (gravity) allow potential energy PE = mgh; total mechanical energy E = KE + PE is conserved without friction. Power P = W/t = F v. Example: lifting 1 kg by 2 m does about 19.6 J of work against gravity.

Vectors and Kinematics. Displacement, velocity, acceleration are vectors. Average velocity = displacement/time. Projectile motion: horizontal velocity constant (no air resistance), vertical motion under g. Range R = v^2 sin(2theta)/g on level ground. Diagrams show vector components and parabolic trajectory.
